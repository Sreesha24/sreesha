Overview

SiteManager Analytics helps irrigation and facilities teams at Lowe’s, Target and AT&T manage water use and landscape health across hundreds of sites in the US.

I led the 0→1 design end to end: analyzing existing research, brainstorming features with PMs, and designing the information architecture, user journey and final UI. The platform helps facility managers monitor site health, compare performance and identify recurring issues.

Impact

  • Designed the first analytics experience in SiteManager, from 0→1
  • Shipped the MVP in 3 weeks, from research to final UI
  • Brought 300+ enterprise sites into one view, ranked by what needs attention first
Problem

Problem: Hundreds of sites. No way to see which ones needed attention.

SiteManager had no analytics at all. Teams managing hundreds of sites had to open them one by one to find leaks, offline controllers and overdue surveys, so critical issues were found late, or not at all.

  • Scattered dataSite health lived across separate screens and reports.
  • No priorityEverything looked equally urgent, so nothing was.
  • Doesn’t scaleSite-by-site checks break down past a few dozen sites.

How might we help a portfolio manager know what needs attention in under a minute?

Solution

Information architectureOrganised the whole product around issues, not sites

Instead of a list of sites to click through, the dashboard starts with what’s wrong across the portfolio, then lets you drill into the site and the device behind it.

This one structural decision shaped every screen that followed.

PrioritisationCritical issues surface first, everything else waits

Issues are ranked by severity and impact, so the top of the screen is always the next thing to fix. Status uses both colour and text labels, so it reads at a glance for everyone.

Drill-downFrom 300 sites to one fix in three clicks

Portfolio → site → issue. Each level keeps its context, with charts that explain the trend and survey reports reorganised so field findings are easy to act on.
