      03 / Design decisions

      Key Design Decisions

      3.1. How do we give traders more control without overwhelming beginners?

      Research showed that traders shared the same broad journey, discover, analyze and evaluate, but needed different tools and levels of detail at each stage.

      Trade-off

      What we preservedFamiliar trading workflows to minimize the learning curve

      What we introducedGreater user control at the cost of interaction and technical complexity

      How much flexibility should traders have without making the dashboard harder to use?

      Concept A

      Refined fixed layout

      What worked

      Preserves a familiar interface

      Decision

      Not selected
      Still forces traders into the same arrangement of tools.

      Concept B

      Preset templates

      What worked

      Reduces initial setup effort

      Decision

      Not selected as core model
      Traders remain limited to predefined configurations.

      Concept C Selected

      Modular workspace

      What worked

      Supports different trading styles

      Decision

      Selected
      Allows tools to be organized around individual strategies, at the cost of added setup complexity.

      Why concept C?

      Modular workspaces gave traders control without locking them into predefined layouts.

      The trade-off was increased setup complexity. The design therefore needed customization to remain understandable and approachable, especially for first-time traders.

      3.2. Making complex trading data easier to scan and strategies easier to evaluate

      Research revealed a tension: beginners struggled with dense trading widgets, while experienced traders relied on that depth of information.

      Rather than removing advanced data, I focused on what traders needed to see first, what they needed to compare, and what could remain accessible on demand.

      Rethinking information hierarchy

      Not all trading information needed equal attention

      InformationDecisionWhy
      Key strategy metricsPrioritizeSupport faster risk/return evaluation
      Supporting market dataGroupMake related indicators easier to compare without competing for attention
      Advanced metricsKeep accessibleRetain analytical depth for experts
      Unfamiliar terminologyExplain in contextGuide beginners without interrupting experts

      Applying the hierarchy across trading tools

      Every widget follows the same reading order: market direction first, supporting signals next, actionable levels last.

          3.3. How do we help beginners make informed trading decisions without slowing down experts?

          Research insights

          Why traders needed more support

          Hotjar and Mixpanel revealed drop-offs during first trading sessions. Beginner interviews highlighted difficulties discovering relevant tools, understanding trading terminology, and evaluating unfamiliar strategies.

          01
          Difficulty choosing the right tools

          Beginners couldn't easily tell which widgets were relevant to their trading goals.

          02
          Learning resources were hard to discover

          Traders wanted easier access to educational content and ideas to explore.

          03
          Unfamiliar concepts interrupted evaluation

          Options terminology, scores, and risk metrics made strategies harder for beginners to understand.

          3.4. How do we scale the Design System without more UX debt?

          OptionsPlay had operated for 12 years without a shared component library, resulting in inconsistent UI patterns. After redesigning the trading dashboard, I built its first design system to help turn the new UI into reusable components and support future features without repeating the same inconsistencies.

          A/ Auditing the new UI to extract patterns and variables

          B/ Building Foundations that prevent future inconsistencies

          C/ Reusable components for faster feature creations

          D / Scaling new trading widgets without redesigning their structure

          The question

          How could OptionsPlay introduce more trading widgets without redesigning their structure every time?

          01

          Standardized the shared widget structure

          Defined consistent headers, control bars, content regions and footers, so each widget didn't need a new layout.

          02

          Preserved flexibility for trading-specific content

          Kept the content region adaptable for charts, tables and strategy data instead of forcing every tool into one layout.

          03

          Defined rules for future widget development

          Documented sizing, states, behavior and layout constraints so new widgets follow the established architecture.

          New trading widgets could reuse established layouts and interaction rules, reducing duplicate design and development work while keeping trading-specific functionality flexible.
